**Interpol: The International Police** Interpol is an international law enforcement organization that facilitates cooperation between police agencies across approximately 186 different countries. Any member state may provide information about a criminal or suspect, enabling other countries to assist in locating the individual, and possibly extraditing them. **What Does Interpol Do?** The purpose of Interpol is to provide member states with technical support in international criminal matters. This support takes four main forms:

- Securing a global police communication system to provide member states with vital information, such as wanted notices and terrorism alerts.
- Offering operational data services and databases to police forces, including name databases, fingerprints, DNA samples, and notices.
- Providing operational police support services to national governments dealing with international criminal activity, including a 24/7 command center capable of intervening at any time to assist any member state in crisis.
- Offering training programs for police forces in every member state to prevent and combat cross-border crime.

Interpol has developed initiatives to combat drug trafficking, terrorism, cybercrime, financial crimes, organized crime, and human trafficking. It also helps in locating and arresting fugitives. In each member state, there is an office known as the National Central Bureau (NCB), staffed by their own national police officers. The Interpol office in Jordan is located in the capital, Amman, near the Fifth Circle.

**What is the Interpol Red Notice?** The red notice is an alert issued by Interpol, an organization of police forces from various countries dedicated to combating international crime. The red notice is not an arrest warrant by itself but serves as an alert indicating that a person is wanted by a law enforcement agency in another country. Interpol’s General Secretariat issues red notices based on the request of a member country.

**Different Types of Interpol Notices** Interpol issues different types of color-coded notices to allow global sharing of alerts and information requests between countries. Each notice serves a specific purpose:

- Red Notice: Requests the arrest of an individual wanted for a crime in another country.
- Blue Notice: Requests information about a suspect’s location or identity.
- Green Notice: Alerts member countries about a suspect’s criminal activities.
- Yellow Notice: Used to locate a missing person.
- Black Notice: Indicates a deceased individual.

**Objections to Interpol Red Notices** While Interpol has a mechanism (the Commission for the Control of Interpol Files) for individuals to request the deletion of negative data about them from its databases, the process requires a lawyer familiar with Interpol cases.

**Who Can Be Subject to a Red Notice?** A red notice is issued at the request of a member country to locate and arrest an individual wanted for extradition to the country where criminal charges originated. An individual can be wanted for trial on suspicion of committing a serious crime or to serve a sentence after a final conviction.

**What Does a Red Notice Contain?** A red notice provides details about a wanted person, including:

- Identification details: Photo, physical description, fingerprints, or identity numbers.
- Judicial information: The reason for the arrest request, such as a criminal charge or court ruling.
