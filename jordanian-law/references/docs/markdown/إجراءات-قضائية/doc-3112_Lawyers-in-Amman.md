**Law as a Calling, Not Just a Profession**

Jordanian law defines a lawyer as an “assistant to the judiciary” who strives to provide legal and judicial assistance. However, the legal profession transcends this definition to become a national and ethical calling, where lawyers contribute to achieving justice and protecting the rights of individuals and society.

**The Importance of the Legal Profession in Society**

- **Defending Rights:** Lawyers ensure that every individual has the right to fair legal representation, especially those who may not know how to defend themselves.
- **Facilitating Access to Justice:** Lawyers assist individuals in understanding complex laws and legal procedures, making it easier for them to access justice.
- **Resolving Disputes:** Lawyers play a crucial role in resolving disputes amicably, reducing the number of cases brought before the courts and expediting the litigation process.
- **Providing Legal Consultations:** Lawyers offer legal advice to help individuals understand their rights and legal obligations.

**The Legal Profession in Amman: A Rich History and Continuous Development**

The legal profession in Jordan boasts a rich history dating back to 1950 and has witnessed continuous development through successive legal amendments. The recent amendments in 2014 marked a significant turning point in regulating the profession, expanding the scope of mandatory representation and increasing lawyer fees.

**Legislation Governing the Profession**

The legal profession in Jordan is governed by the Jordanian Bar Association Law, in addition to nine other regulations that govern various aspects of the profession, such as the retirement and social security system and the health insurance system.

**Professional Ethics: The Foundation of Integrity and Respect**

The Jordanian Bar Association adheres to a code of professional conduct and ethics, which outlines the moral and behavioral standards that lawyers must uphold. These standards include honesty, integrity, and mutual respect between lawyers, judges, and other parties involved in the judicial process.
