**Key Definitions**

- **Compensation:** Compensation refers to the monetary or in-kind reparation awarded to an injured party to redress the harm suffered due to another party’s wrongful act.
- **Compensation Cases:** These are lawsuits filed by individuals who have suffered harm caused by others, whether individuals or entities like insurance companies, governments, or corporations. The cases are brought before the competent judicial authority to seek compensation for all damages incurred.
- **Compensation Lawyer:** A compensation lawyer is qualified to represent clients in matters related to obtaining compensation, including filing and pursuing legal claims.
- **Damage:** Damage encompasses any harm inflicted upon a person’s rights or legitimate interests. It is a fundamental element in establishing liability, as liability entails an obligation to compensate, and compensation is assessed based on the extent of the damage.

  - **Material Damage:** This refers to tangible financial losses, such as property damage, lost business opportunities, or medical expenses resulting from an injury.
  - **Moral Damage:** This encompasses non-financial harm, such as pain and suffering resulting from an injury or emotional distress.

**Legitimacy of Seeking Compensation**

The Egyptian Dar al-Ifta has affirmed the permissibility of obtaining compensation awarded by courts to victims of accidents and other incidents. It also clarified that legal fees charged by lawyers in compensation cases are permissible, provided the lawyer performs their duties with honesty and integrity.

**Purpose of Compensation**

While criminal liability aims to deter and punish the offender, civil liability focuses on rectifying the harm and restoring the injured party to their pre-harm state. The fundamental principle governing compensation is proportionality between the damage and the compensation awarded. The compensation should be equivalent to the damage, neither exceeding nor falling short.
